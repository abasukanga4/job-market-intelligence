# Decision memo: what this sample supports

## The question

Which kinds of data roles appeared in my collected feed, and is there enough evidence to change my application strategy?

## What I found

The snapshot has 519 records collected over 16 calendar days in May-June 2026. Title rules classify 119 as Data Analyst and 69 as Data Scientist. That makes DA worth examining, but it does not establish entry-level eligibility. The Other category has 142 records and the source mix is {'indeed': 495, 'linkedin': 24}.

Data quality changes how I interpret those counts. Posting dates are missing for 441 records. There are 105 repeated source/title/company/location groups. Keeping the first row from each key would leave 393 rows, but that is only a sensitivity scenario: it could discard separate genuine openings.

Only 113 records have an unambiguous hybrid or remote label. A further 105 have mixed labels. Neither group establishes that the location or work rights suit me.

## What I cannot conclude

The 121 historical scores of at least 70 are not verified suitable vacancies. The model, prompt and CV version are not recorded in the public data, and no interview outcomes are available to evaluate the scores. I cannot use them to show that application volume is the bottleneck, or that my capabilities already meet the adverts.

Recruiter adverts also need checking individually. Counting posts from a few named firms does not measure the number of invalid opportunities in the feed.

## The decision

I would test a DA-focused shortlist as a hypothesis, not commit to a fixed increase in applications on these figures alone. First I would review full adverts, distinguish essential from desirable requirements, check location and work rights, and investigate repeated records. Then I would track applications and interview outcomes across comparable role groups.

The immediate improvement is better screening and measurement. This snapshot does not identify a single cause of unsuccessful applications.
